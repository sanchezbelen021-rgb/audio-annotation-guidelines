
# Audio Annotation Guidelines
## Latin American Spanish

### 1. Introduction

This repository contains a practical reference guide for audio annotation and transcription quality assessment, with a focus on Latin American Spanish.

It is an independent portfolio project created to demonstrate language expertise, attention to detail, and understanding of audio annotation workflows.

### 2. Transcription Guidelines

- Transcribe exactly what the speaker says.
- Preserve the original meaning and wording.
- Do not paraphrase or summarize spoken content.
- Use standard spelling and grammar conventions.
- Do not add words that cannot be clearly heard.

### 3. Punctuation and Capitalization

- Use punctuation according to the speaker's intended meaning.
- Capitalize the first word of each sentence.
- Use question marks for questions.
- Use exclamation marks when the speaker clearly expresses an exclamation.
- Avoid unnecessary punctuation.

### 4. Disfluencies and Repetitions

- Preserve relevant repetitions.
- Include spoken fillers when required by the annotation guidelines.
- Do not silently correct grammatical mistakes.
- Maintain the natural structure of spontaneous speech.

Examples:
- "Eh, bueno, quería consultar..."
- "Yo, yo creo que sí."
- "Este... no estoy segura."

### 5. Speaker Identification

- Identify different speakers consistently.
- Assign a unique speaker label to each voice.
- Do not assume speaker identity based on context.
- Mark speaker changes when clearly audible.

### 6. Audio Quality Issues

Pay attention to:
- Background noise.
- Overlapping speech.
- Low recording volume.
- Distorted or unclear audio.
- Missing or incomplete words.

Never guess content that cannot be reliably understood.

### 7. Quality Assurance Checklist

Before submitting an annotation, verify:

- [ ] All audible speech has been reviewed.
- [ ] Words have been transcribed accurately.
- [ ] Punctuation and capitalization are consistent.
- [ ] Repetitions and relevant fillers are preserved.
- [ ] Speaker labels are consistent.
- [ ] Unclear sections have not been guessed.
- [ ] The final transcript has been reviewed.

### 8. Common Annotation Mistakes

- Rewriting spoken language instead of transcribing it.
- Omitting relevant repetitions.
- Guessing unclear words.
- Inconsistent punctuation.
- Incorrect speaker identification.
- Ignoring background noise or overlapping speech.

### 9. Scope and Disclaimer

These guidelines are illustrative and intended for portfolio demonstration purposes.

Actual annotation projects may have specific instructions that take precedence over these general recommendations.
